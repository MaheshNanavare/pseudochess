# Inverted Gravity

A visual philosophy for the PseudoChess icon and Store art, written with the canvas-design skill.

**Order, held upside down.** Inverted Gravity treats a strict system (a grid, a rank of identical forms, a single authority) and then quietly reverses its pull. Weight rises. Emptiness is the prize, not the loss. Nothing is broken or chaotic: the grid stays exact and every edge stays true. Only the direction of meaning has turned, the way a room looks when you lie on the floor and stare at the ceiling. The work should feel meticulously crafted, the product of painstaking attention to the smallest alignment, so the inversion reads as deliberate and never as an error.

**Space and form.** Composition is built on an eight-part module. Forms are solid, geometric and few: squares that behave like tiles, rounded bars, a single sovereign silhouette. Negative space is not background; it is the destination the whole composition drains towards. Where marks accumulate, they thin out along one axis until they vanish, so the eye reads a direction of travel without any arrow. Every interval is measured, the product of master-level execution and countless refinements, never eyeballed.

**Colour and material.** A night palette: deep aubergine ink as the ground, dusk violet and lilac mist as the only tones, and one marigold accent used once, where obligation lives. The accent is small and absolute, a single point of heat in a cool field. Surfaces are flat and matte, like enamel on metal; no gradients for decoration, no glows, no textures that pretend to be paper. The restraint itself should read as the result of deep expertise.

**Scale and rhythm.** One element is monumental and everything else is modest. Repetition is the rhythm: identical small forms in a row, spaced with metronomic precision, then released one by one into the empty field. The viewer should sense a countdown without seeing a number. Scale contrast carries hierarchy, so nothing needs to shout.

**Text as a whisper.** Words appear only when they anchor what the image already says: a name, or a few words at most, set small with generous tracking and placed on the grid like any other tile. The typography is part of the construction, aligned to the same module as the shapes. Every letter-space is checked, every margin held, so the whole reads as one meticulously crafted object from someone at the top of their field.
